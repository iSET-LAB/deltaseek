Title: Deviation-Seeking Viewpoint Planning for Robotic As-Built Verification
Abstract
A growing body of construction robotics research uses Building Information Models as a geometric prior for navigation and inspection. Existing as-built verification pipelines collect data along preset or coverage-driven routes and compare against the model offline, so the verification objective never informs where the robot looks; recent active-sensing work does close this loop, but treats model–reality divergence as a safety signal for reaching a navigation goal. We instead make deviation detection the planning objective itself. We present a deviation-seeking viewpoint planning framework for a mobile manipulator performing as-built verification. Rather than minimizing traversal cost to a goal, our planner spends a traversal budget to maximize expected information about the deviation state of modeled elements, selecting base positions and arm configurations that resolve ambiguous elements. Including the manipulator in viewpoint selection reaches vantage points unavailable to a fixed-sensor platform — looking behind and beneath partially occluded assemblies, into low equipment enclosures, and around corners without repositioning the base. We evaluate in Gazebo on a Clearpath Husky A300 with a UR5e arm, in worlds derived from IFC models into which deviations of known type, location, and magnitude are programmatically injected, providing exact ground truth and controllable deviation density. Against coverage-based inspection, frontier exploration, and goal-directed navigation, our planner detects [X]% of injected deviations within [Y]% of the traversal distance. We are extending the evaluation to the physical platform to assess robustness under real localization drift and sensor noise.
